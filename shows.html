Hands-On Exposure to the Latest Technology

One of the most compelling reasons to attend an arable agriculture show is the unparalleled access it provides to the latest farming technology — not in a catalogue or on a website, but in person and often in operation. Equipment manufacturers and technology companies use these events to launch and demonstrate their newest products, from precision planting machinery and GPS-guided tractors to drone spraying systems, soil moisture sensors, and grain handling equipment. Seeing a piece of machinery working in real conditions is fundamentally different from reading its specifications on paper. Farmers can ask direct questions of engineers and product specialists, compare competing brands side by side, and in many cases take equipment for a demonstration run. This hands-on experience dramatically improves the quality of purchasing decisions, helping farmers invest in technology that genuinely fits their operation rather than being guided purely by advertising. For farmers who operate in remote areas and rarely visit large towns, a single show visit can expose them to several years' worth of technological development in one afternoon.

Access to Cutting-Edge Agronomic Knowledge

Agriculture shows typically host a packed programme of seminars, panel discussions, and live demonstrations led by agronomists, researchers, plant breeders, and extension officers. These sessions cover everything from the latest findings on fertiliser efficiency and integrated pest management to emerging threats like new fall armyworm strains or fungal diseases, and climate adaptation strategies tailored to specific agro-ecological zones. This kind of knowledge is often at the frontier of agricultural science — information that has not yet filtered through to local extension services or farming publications. Farmers who attend these sessions return to their land equipped with insights that give them a genuine competitive advantage. More importantly, the interactive format of show seminars — where farmers can ask questions and engage in discussion — makes the learning far more relevant and memorable than reading a circular or attending a formal lecture.

Networking With Fellow Farmers

The value of the conversations that happen between farmers at an agriculture show is difficult to overstate. Farming can be an isolating profession — long hours on the land, geographic distance from neighbours, and the sheer busyness of the season can leave farmers without regular contact with peers who understand their challenges. Agriculture shows break down that isolation and create a rare gathering of people who speak the same language. These informal exchanges — at the demonstration plot, over lunch, or while walking between exhibitor stands — are where some of the most valuable knowledge transfers happen. A farmer from one district discovers how a peer in another district is managing waterlogging. A young farmer gets mentored by an experienced grower who offers to visit their land. A group of smallholders who meet at a show realise they have enough combined volume to negotiate better input prices as a collective. These relationships and the knowledge they carry often outlast anything that happens in a formal seminar.

Digoronto Agric-Shop was founded with a single, clear purpose to make quality agricultural inputs accessible to every farmer in Botswana, whether they till one hectare or one hundred. The name "Digoronto" — meaning fruits in Setswana — reflects our inspiration from the desire to symbolize the ultimate results of a harvest and the hardworking farmers who feed our communities. Starting from our flagship branch in Moshupa, we have grown steadily by listening to farmers, understanding their unique challenges and stocking solutions that actually work in Botswana's climate and soils.
