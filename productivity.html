How to Accelerate Agricultural Productivity in Arable Agriculture

Start with the soil

Productivity in arable agriculture begins and ends with soil health. No amount of good seed or expensive fertiliser will deliver consistent yields on degraded, compacted, or nutrient-depleted land. The foundation of any productivity improvement strategy must therefore be a commitment to understanding and rehabilitating the soil. Farmers should begin with a proper soil test before every planting season to determine pH levels, organic matter content, and available nutrients. Lime application to correct acidic soils can unlock nutrients that are already present but chemically unavailable to plant roots — a simple and relatively inexpensive intervention that often produces dramatic yield improvements. Building organic matter through compost incorporation, green manuring, and the retention of crop residues improves soil structure over time, enhancing water infiltration, aeration, and microbial activity. Healthy, biologically active soil is the single greatest long-term productivity asset any arable farmer can invest in.

Use Certified Seed Varieties

One of the fastest and most cost-effective ways to accelerate productivity is to move from recycled or local landrace seed to certified, improved varieties developed specifically for the target environment. Modern improved varieties of maize, sorghum, sunflower, cowpea, and groundnut offer significantly higher yield potential, better disease resistance, and in many cases improved drought tolerance compared to traditional varieties. Farmers should select varieties that are recommended for their specific agro-ecological zone, paying attention to maturity period — early-maturing varieties are particularly important in areas with short or unpredictable rainfall seasons. Seed should always be sourced from reputable agro-dealers or certified seed companies to guarantee germination rates and variety purity. Investing in quality seed is arguably the highest-return input decision an arable farmer can make.

Optimise Planting Timing and Population

Planting at the right time and at the correct plant population density are two of the most underestimated productivity levers in arable farming. Late planting exposes crops to end-of-season moisture stress during critical grain-filling periods, while thin plant populations leave productive land underutilised and allow weeds to establish unchallenged. Farmers should aim to plant within the recommended planting window for their crop and region — typically within the first two weeks of reliable rainfall onset. Correct row spacing and inter-plant spacing, as specified on the seed label or by an agronomist, ensure that each plant has access to adequate light, moisture, and nutrients without competing unnecessarily with its neighbours. Getting these basics right costs nothing extra but can improve yields substantially.
